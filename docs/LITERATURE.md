Review date: 27 September 2026. Scope: **all rank-4, 11-element excluded minors for GF(5)-representability, up to matroid isomorphism**.

**The target appears open in the public literature located in this review.** I found no theorem or catalogue claiming this specific classification. The strongest directly relevant published enumeration located stops at ten elements in unrestricted rank. This is a literature-based assessment, not a proof that no unpublished or unindexed computation exists. Before claiming novelty in a paper, a focused enquiry to Brettell and Campbell would be useful; no enquiry has been sent.

The search covered “rank-4”, “rank four”, “rank-four”, “11-element”, “11 elements”, “eleven”, “GF(5)”, “quinary”, “excluded minors”, and recent-year combinations, together with arXiv, journal records, author publication/code pages, and the Matroid Union reports. Search-engine crawl dates were not treated as publication dates.

The following papers and first-person research reports provide the relevant evidence and methods.

1. **Nick Brettell, *The excluded minors for GF(5)-representable matroids on ten elements*.** Advances in Applied Mathematics **166** (2025), 102864; preprint first posted in 2023, revised 8 October 2024. Theorem 1.1 gives 2,128 excluded minors on exactly ten elements. Table 9 gives the rank distribution below. The enumeration descends the Hydra hierarchy. Its result through 13 elements concerns Hydra-5, not GF(5). Consequently that larger size bound does not settle our target. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14614v2), [published version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2025.102864).

   | Size | Rank 4 | All ranks |
   | --- | ---: | ---: |
   | 7 | 5 | 12 |
   | 8 | 92 | 96 |
   | 9 | 219 | 456 |
   | 10 | 130 | 2,128 |

   There are 564 through size nine and 2,692 through size ten. The 130 in rank 4 and the 130 in rank 6 are separate dual classes in these counts. The rank-four slices of sizes 7–10 have now all been independently reproduced in this workspace and compared exactly by isomorphism with the published archive.

2. **Dillon Mayhew and Gordon Royle, *Matroids with nine elements*.** Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B **98** (2008), 415–431. Their orderly computation and catalogue are the earlier general baseline. The paper reports 383,172 matroids on **exactly** nine elements; this should not be confused with a cumulative count through nine. It also illustrates the rapid increase in general matroid enumeration. For this project, the catalogue can support independent small-instance checks and test isomorphism handling. [Author PDF](https://dillon128.github.io/MR08.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2007.07.005).

3. **Anton Betten, R. J. Kingan and S. R. Kingan, *A note on GF(5)-representable matroids*.** MATCH **58** (2007), 511–521. They enumerate rank-3 excluded minors through twelve elements, finding one of size ten and one of size eleven, and none of size twelve. Their filtering separates a forbidden rank-2 minor, representability, and deletion minimality, with canonical labeling for comparisons. Their bounded enumeration alone is not an all-size rank-3 theorem. [Journal PDF](https://match.pmf.kg.ac.rs/electronic_versions/Match58/n2/match58n2_511-521.pdf).

4. **Rutger Campbell, *The rank-3 excluded minors for GF(5)-representability* (26 October 2020).** This first-person report describes joint supervision with Jim Geelen of Timothy Wahyudi's coding project. It reports the complete rank-3 list of 18, distributed as 5, 2, 9, 1, 1 at sizes 7–11, with none larger. The search tracks inequivalent representations and uses stabilizers to bound how long representations can persist under extension. It is an author report, not a journal article located in this review. The rank-3 result does not classify rank 4. The representation tracking and its completeness argument are valuable precedents. [Report](https://matroidunion.org/?p=3244).

5. **Nick Brettell and Rudi Pendavingh, *Computing excluded minors for classes of matroids representable over partial fields*.** Electronic Journal of Combinatorics **31**(3) (2024), P3.20. Sections 3–4 describe finite-field proxies, strong stabilizers, orderly generation, minor checking, and splicing compatible extensions over a common deletion. This is the most directly useful implementation reference. Its code was written for Sage 8.1, and the paper reports computations using four cores and 23 GB RAM. It enumerates dyadic and 2-regular obstructions through fifteen elements; these are different representation classes. The revised paper also records later theory completing the 2-regular characterization, so the original abstract's conjectural wording is outdated. [Revised paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.13175v2).

6. **R. A. Pendavingh and S. H. M. van Zwam, *Confinement of matroid representations to subsets of partial fields*.** Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B **100** (2010), 510–545. This supplies the theoretical basis for the Hydra hierarchy. Under the stated 3-connectivity and minor hypotheses, Hydra representability records the number of inequivalent GF(5) representations. The companion *The Hydra-k partial fields* includes Mathematica computations. These results suggest future reductions, but their hypotheses must be checked before using them to discard parents or representations. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4487), [computational companion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1640).

7. **Petr Hliněný, *Equivalence-free exhaustive generation of matroid representations*.** Discrete Applied Mathematics **154** (2006), 1210–1222. This develops a canonical construction path for represented 3-connected matroids with a given minor and describes its implementation in MACEK. It is relevant to future parent enumeration. Represented equivalence and abstract matroid isomorphism have to be distinguished, particularly when representations are not unique. MACEK is an alternative/reference implementation; it has not been installed here. [Author preprint](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~hlineny/papers/genmat-new.pdf), [MACEK](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~hlineny/MACEK/macek.html).

8. **Markus Kirchweger, Manfred Scheucher and Stefan Szeider, *A SAT Attack on Rota's Basis Conjecture*.** SAT 2022, LIPIcs **236**, 4:1–4:18. Section 4.1 encodes a matroid by Boolean basis variables and all ordered basis-exchange constraints. The work combines SAT with symmetry handling. This is a concrete precedent for SAT enumeration of abstract matroids, not a GF(5) excluded-minor classification. Our initial extension generator uses the standard basis-axiom encoding, without importing their symmetry machinery. Rota's **basis** conjecture is a different question from the finite-field excluded-minor conjecture. [Paper and metadata](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.4).

The [author's code page](https://homepages.ecs.vuw.ac.nz/~bretteni/code.html) provides the smaller excluded-minor catalogue and Sage source. Its prose mentions 2,128, but its own loading example expects **2,692 total**, of which 2,128 have size ten. The linked filename is `gf5_excluded_minors10.sobj`, although the example omits `gf5_`. The cached generator contains Python 2 syntax (`print` statements and `.iteritems()`), so it is not assumed to run unchanged on the installed Sage 10.9. Reference downloads are kept inert and checksummed; the new prototype uses its own small implementation.

The resulting search uses complete represented-parent catalogues and compatible deletion tables, with both SAT and an independent finite-table join implementation.
